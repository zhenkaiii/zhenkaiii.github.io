Stay Sharp — Interview Maintenance Routine

2 Saturdays / month · ~10 hrs total · Never start from zero again

The Problem

You built a 408KB PyTorch K-means notebook. You nailed it at LinkedIn and Meta. Three months later at Netflix, you couldn't finish the code in 15 minutes and said the complexity was N log N instead of N*K*D.

Preparation builds the muscle. But muscle that hasn't been activated in 3 months is stiff. You don't need to rebuild it — you need to stretch it regularly. The notebook isn't wasted because you couldn't recall it 3 months later — it's the reason you'll only need 10 minutes to reactivate instead of hours.

Knowledge decays. Templates decay. Verbal fluency decays. The only thing that doesn't decay is the infrastructure you've built (fundamentals pages, prep sheets, notebooks). But infrastructure without regular activation is a library you can't navigate in the dark.

Don't trust past preparation. Trust same-week activation. The warmup window is days, not months.

The Routine: 2 Saturdays / Month

Saturday 1: Coding (3 hours)

Open a blank file. No references. Write from memory. Time yourself.

Remaining time: Pick one template that felt rusty. Trace through an example input by hand. Write edge cases. Update your fundamentals page if anything was wrong.

Saturday 2: Concepts + Story (2 hours)

Why This Works

You went from intern to full-time conversion. You never did the cold interview grind. The Netflix round was your first real interview loop. Scoring ~7/10 at Netflix on your first real attempt is the starting point, not the ceiling.

The people who seem effortlessly sharp in interviews have done this 5, 10, 20 times. They've bombed VAE questions before. They've run out of time on coding before. They just did it years ago and built the scar tissue.

You're building that scar tissue now. The fundamentals pages, the gaps doc, the mock recordings, the PyTorch notebooks — that's your infrastructure. Most people don't build that. They cram for 2 weeks, interview, forget everything, and start from zero next time.

You won't start from zero next time. You'll open your own pages, warm up for a day, and walk in sharper than R1.
